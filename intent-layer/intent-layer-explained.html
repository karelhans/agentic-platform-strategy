What the Intent Layer is, and where it sits

Spec-driven development (SDD) writes down what a product must do before anyone builds it. The Intent Layer sits one step earlier. It writes down what the business is trying to achieve, so every spec can be traced back to a reason a banker would recognise.

A spec says what to build

Screens, data, behaviour, acceptance criteria. It belongs to one product and changes when the product changes.

Intent says why it matters

The outcome the business wants, the job a banker is trying to get done, the judgement that stays with a human. It stays true if the product disappears.

1. Where it sits

Evidence from bankers flows in on the left. Records flow out to the people writing specs on the right. Products are tested against the layer. They never write to it.

2. What one record chain looks like

The layer is organised by job family. A job family is one standing job a senior banker does, such as triaging intelligence. Each job family is documented as a chain of six record types that answer six different questions. This is the real intelligence chain, with its actual IDs.

There are four job families today, all for Senior Coverage MDs: intelligence, client relationships, opportunity pipeline, and consequential client meetings. A fifth, actions and commitments, is a working hypothesis. Each job family has exactly this shape.

3. How much to trust a record

Every record carries a maturity label. It describes the evidence behind the record, never how far along a product is. A workspace may build on evidence-backed or confirmed records. A hypothesis may inform research but cannot justify a build on its own.

Read the label, then read the count. The five confirmed job statements today were each accepted by one Senior Coverage MD. Until a second and third banker has been through them, the charter tells consumers to treat them as evidence-backed, single participant.

4. The litmus test

This is what the layer is for. Before build, an initiative writes down which records it serves. If it cannot name them, it has not yet shown that it adds value. The first three tests are pass or fail. The rest attach conditions.

Six words you will meet

Job family
One standing job a senior banker does. The unit the catalogue is organised by. Four exist today, all for Coverage.
JTBD
Job to be done. A one-sentence statement of the progress a banker wants, in the shape "when X, help me Y, so that Z, without W". It stays true if the product disappears.
Value delivered
What one completed run of a use case leaves behind. For intelligence triage it is a decision, its reason, and a destination.
Scenario
The same use case under different conditions. Not a screen, not a feature. If the value delivered changes, it is a new use case.
Maturity
How much evidence stands behind a record. Hypothesis, evidence-backed, confirmed, superseded. Never delivery status.
Review trigger
What you raise when your evidence contradicts a record. The record is marked for review; you do not edit it or work around it.

What is in the layer today

Coverage, four job families

Intelligence, relationships, pipeline, meetings. Each with outcome, family, use case, JTBD and scenarios. Two actor records. Everything cross-referenced by ID.

One participant

All confirmed statements rest on one Senior Coverage MD. Eight proxy-interview guides are drafted to widen this; none has been run yet.

No numbers yet

Every business outcome has its baseline and target marked for validation. The first job of the next interview round is to put ranges on them.

Coverage only

ECM, DCM, M&A, Sponsors and GCB have no intent records. Silence in the scope table means no coverage, not implied coverage.

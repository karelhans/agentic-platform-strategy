What a full job family looks like

The catalogue today holds one business use case and one job to be done per job family. This page shows the shape the records are designed for: several use cases under one family, jobs that explain more than one use case, and product use cases citing steps from below the line.

1. The generic shape

Read top to bottom. The outcome says what should change. The family is the standing area of work. The use cases are the processes inside it, each with six steps and a value delivered. Jobs to be done sit beside the use cases and explain why the work is done; the relationship is many-to-many. Scenarios hang under each use case. Actors say who judges and who contributes. Everything under the dashed line is a product's, and it only cites.

2. The intelligence family with the IBIQ use cases mapped on

The five IBIQ product specs were each read against the catalogue under the charter's rules. Two of them stay inside intelligence triage. Three cross into relationships, pipeline and meetings. Two expose a business use case the catalogue does not have, and all five need an actor it does not have. Those are shown as candidates, dashed and amber, because their only source so far is a product spec.

Why this matters for the model. The assessment said the job family record was doing no work because every family held exactly one use case. Orient My Day and Draft Outreach are the first signals that a family can hold more than one: both produce a different value delivered from the record they would otherwise cite. The signal comes from product reasoning, so it is a reason to go and look for the episode, not a reason to add the record.

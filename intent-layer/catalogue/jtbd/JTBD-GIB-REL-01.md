| **Job statement** | When managing a priority client institution, help me judge and strengthen the quality of the relationship using meaningful markers of trust, access, reciprocity, and engagement, informed by what we understand of the client's evolving agenda, so JPM remains relevant, trusted, and positioned to influence and compete. |
| **Situation and triggers** | A priority relationship requires recurring review or material context, trajectory, commitment, timing, or risk changes. |
| **Desired progress** | Move from fragmented or activity-led relationship understanding to an accepted institution-level quality judgment and purposeful disposition. |
| **Required judgment** | Reading relationship quality and direction from markers rather than from contact volume; judging whether and where senior attention changes the result; choosing the purpose of any engagement and whether to engage personally, delegate, orchestrate JPM, repair, or deliberately wait. |
| **Desired outcome** | JPM remains relevant, trusted, and positioned to influence and compete across the client institution. |
| **Unwanted trade-offs** | Contact without a client purpose, created only because a cadence gap or dry spell exists (Q33, Q99); client fatigue from contact that is frequent rather than meaningful (Q33, Q99); false precision about the client's agenda, which is evolving and hard to document and must be inferred through markers rather than modelled as settled (Q110); duplicated or uncoordinated outreach from several parts of JPM to one institution (Q94). |

| **Related scenarios** | `SC-GIB-REL-01-A` through `SC-GIB-REL-01-D`. |
| **Success signals** | Candor, access, client-initiated requests for JPM's view, reciprocal commitments, meaningful contact, and resilient relevance. |

## Interviewer Guidance

- Share all twelve questions in advance.
- Ask respondents to label answers as direct experience, observed practice, or informed hypothesis.
- Keep the lens at institution-level relationship intent; use the separate Meeting, Pipeline, Actions, and Intelligence interviews for their bounded jobs.
- Do not turn relationship quality into one opaque score or equate activity volume with strength.
- Preserve variation by client tier, trajectory, region, sector, and Coverage model.
- Proxy responses establish candidate direction only. They do not reconfirm banker intent.

## Expected Direction-Setting Output

The proxy round should produce:

1. The primary progress and boundary of relationship management.
2. A candidate set of meaningful relationship-quality markers.
3. Attention, cadence, stakeholder, and engagement rules.
4. A Coverage-led orchestration and contributor model.
5. Deliberate non-action and handoff boundaries.
6. Measures, guardrails, disagreements, and banker-validation questions.

## CREL01 - Primary Progress

**Question:** What should senior relationship management enable a Coverage MD to create or protect more reliably?

**Response mode:** Select and rank up to three primary outcomes; classify other selected items as enablers or downstream benefits.

**Possible answers:**

- Trusted access to the client institution.
- Better understanding of the client's evolving agenda.
- Permission to influence consequential decisions.
- The right to compete for priority opportunities.
- Broader and more resilient institutional relevance.
- Reciprocal and reliable commitments.
- Coherent JPM engagement across products and regions.
- Early recognition and repair of relationship risk.
- Another outcome.

## CREL02 - Unit And Scope

**Question:** What should be the primary unit of relationship judgment, and where should the boundary sit with stakeholder, opportunity, meeting, and action work?

**Response mode:** Choose the primary unit, then classify adjacent work as included context, linked work, or out of scope.

**Possible answers:**

- Client institution.
- Individual stakeholder.
- Relationship objective.
- Connected stakeholder network.
- Live client situation.
- Adaptive by client type or objective.
- Another unit.

**Boundary prompts:** stakeholder engagement, opportunity pursuit, meeting preparation, commitment execution, market or client intelligence, and cross-bank coordination.

## CREL03 - Relationship-Quality Markers

**Question:** Which observable markers best indicate genuine relationship quality rather than contact volume or sentiment alone?

**Response mode:** Rank the five most meaningful markers and identify misleading proxies.

**Possible answers:**

- Candor and willingness to share difficult context.
- Access to relevant senior decision-makers.
- Client-initiated requests for JPM perspective.
- Reciprocal commitments that resolve reliably.
- JPM influence on client thinking or decisions.
- Breadth and resilience of stakeholder access.
- Inclusion in consequential moments.
- Recovery after setbacks or difficult conversations.
- Meaningful rather than merely frequent contact.
- Another marker.

## CREL04 - Understanding The Client Agenda

**Question:** How should Coverage understand the client's evolving agenda without pretending it can be fully documented or reduced to a static model?

**Response mode:** Select the evidence that should inform judgment and identify what must remain a hypothesis.

**Possible answers:**

- Explicit client statements and commitments.
- Strategic decisions and capital allocation.
- Leadership, board, ownership, or stakeholder changes.
- Interaction patterns, questions, and requests for perspective.
- Market, sector, competitive, and regulatory context.
- Active opportunities, risks, and dependencies.
- Product and regional observations across JPM.
- Coverage hypotheses with visible evidence and uncertainty.
- Another source.

## CREL05 - Senior Attention Trigger

**Question:** What should cause a relationship issue or opportunity to rise specifically to Senior Coverage MD attention?

**Response mode:** Select and rank the strongest tests; explain how tier and trajectory change them.

**Possible answers:**

- Relationship leverage where MD involvement can alter the result.
- Material relationship risk or loss of trust.
- A forming client decision.
- A significant JPM or client commitment.
- Need for cross-JPM orchestration.
- Seniority, protocol, or right-to-engage considerations.
- A material change in client context or trajectory.
- A cadence condition for a priority relationship.
- Another trigger.

## CREL06 - Stakeholder Selection

**Question:** For a specific relationship objective, what should determine which client stakeholder and JPM participants are involved?

**Response mode:** Select the governing tests and identify who resolves conflicts.

**Possible answers:**

- Ability to shape the intended client outcome.
- Possession of relevant context or authority.
- Relationship quality or risk requiring attention.
- Ability to broaden access or institutional relevance.
- Connection to a material commitment.
- Credible JPM right to engage.
- MD presence materially changes the result.
- Need to rebalance an over-concentrated stakeholder portfolio.
- Protocol, sensitivity, or regional constraints.
- Another test.

## CREL07 - Purposeful Engagement

**Question:** What must be clear before senior relationship engagement is warranted?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum engagement thesis and identify what may remain on demand.

**Possible answers:**

- What changed and why the moment matters.
- What matters to the stakeholder or institution.
- The intended learning, influence, repair, or commitment outcome.
- Why JPM has a credible right to engage.
- What perspective, question, or message to bring.
- What must be avoided or treated sensitively.
- Desired JPM participants and posture.
- What should happen next if the interaction succeeds.
- Another element.

## CREL08 - Institution-Level Orchestration

**Question:** How should Coverage orchestrate one coherent institution relationship across product, regional, and specialist teams?

**Response mode:** Choose the closest accountability model and explain conflict resolution.

**Possible answers:**

- Coverage MD owns institution-level coherence and coordinates contributors.
- Best-positioned banker leads each situation under a Coverage framework.
- Shared senior ownership with one named coordinator.
- Ownership varies by product, geography, or client objective.
- Product teams own product agendas while Coverage owns sequencing and client posture.
- Another model.

**Probes:** duplicate outreach, conflicting positioning, hidden activity, commitment ownership, and escalation to relevant business heads.

## CREL09 - Cadence And Deliberate Non-Action

**Question:** When should cadence trigger review, delegated contact, personal MD engagement, deliberate monitoring, or no action?

**Response mode:** Define the rule by relationship tier and trajectory.

**Possible answers:**

- Cadence triggers review but never contact by itself.
- Cadence may trigger delegated maintenance.
- Cadence may warrant personal MD contact for priority relationships.
- Changed trajectory matters more than elapsed time.
- A clear purpose and right to engage are always required.
- Listening or maintaining access can itself be a valid purpose.
- Deliberate wait is valid with a reassessment condition.
- Another rule.

## CREL10 - Prepared Case And Ownership

**Question:** What should be assembled before a relationship issue reaches the MD, and what should leave the review with an owner?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum prepared case and completion contract.

**Possible answers:**

- Institution-level change and trajectory.
- Relationship-quality markers and contradictions.
- Reason for attention now.
- Relevant stakeholder implications.
- Cross-JPM context and planned engagement.
- Recommended objective and engagement options.
- Uncertainty, sensitivity, and right-to-engage constraints.
- Accepted disposition, accountable owner, and next movement.
- Reassessment condition if waiting.
- Another element.

## CREL11 - Success And Failure

**Question:** What would prove relationship management is improving the business, and what must it avoid?

**Response mode:** Select one or two leading measures, one or two lagging outcomes, and the most important guardrails.

**Possible success measures:**

- Increased candor and useful access.
- More client-initiated requests for JPM's view.
- Reciprocal commitments resolving reliably.
- Earlier recognition and repair of relationship risk.
- Broader relevant stakeholder access.
- Greater relevance and right to compete.
- Coherent cross-JPM engagement.

**Possible failure modes:**

- Treating contact frequency as relationship quality.
- Opaque scoring without explainable markers.
- Purposeless or duplicative outreach.
- Over-contacting the client.
- Fragmented JPM posture.
- Missing or unowned commitments.
- False certainty about the client's agenda.

## CREL12 - Representative Episode

**Question:** Describe a recent relationship episode in which trust, access, relevance, or the right to compete materially improved or deteriorated. What changed, how was it recognized, and what did Coverage do?

**Response mode:** Use a direct experience where possible; otherwise label the example as observed practice or hypothesis.

**Probes:**

- Which markers revealed the change?
- Which stakeholder and institution context mattered?
- What was the relationship objective?
- Who orchestrated JPM and who owned follow-through?
- Was personal engagement, delegation, coordination, repair, or deliberate wait chosen?
- What would have improved the outcome?

## Proxy Synthesis And Banker Validation Gate

After proxy interviews, synthesize the relationship-quality marker set, attention and cadence rules, institution-orchestration model, prepared-case contract, measures, disagreements, and evidence gaps. Current bankers should receive no more than five focused validation questions, including one concrete episode and any disputed marker, tier, or authority rule.

# Coverage Intent Model Validation - Proxy Interview

## Interview Record

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Interview ID** | `INT-COV-MV-001` |
| **Question range** | MV00-MV11 |
| **Status** | `Draft` |
| **Direction-setting audience** | Former senior Coverage bankers (Line MD or Group Head level), former Coverage VPs who ran a senior's book, and product proxies with direct Coverage exposure |
| **Focused validation audience** | At least two current Senior Coverage MDs who did not take part in Round 1, plus one product banker, after proxy synthesis |
| **Purpose** | Test whether the existing four-lens Coverage intent model is the right summary of how Coverage operates and thinks, and whether it is framed around value, before current-banker time is spent. This guide validates what exists. It does not set new direction and does not produce new JTBDs. |
| **Existing foundation** | Five JTBD statements (intelligence triage, client relationship management, opportunity pipeline stewardship, consequential client meetings, umbrella), one working hypothesis (actions and commitments), four business use cases with six steps each, fourteen scenarios, and two actor cohorts. All derived from one Senior Coverage MD, Q01 to Q151. Every statement in the pre-read pack is a hypothesis for this interview, whatever its catalogue maturity. |
| **What this guide is not** | Not a discovery interview, not a market-intelligence direction interview, not a prototype review. If a respondent starts describing tools, bring them back to the work. |

## Interviewer Guidance

- Send the pre-read pack (Appendix A) at least 48 hours ahead. Ask respondents to mark it up before the session.
- Ask MV00, the episode, first and in full before showing any statement. Do not let the model shape the story.
- Ask respondents to label every answer as direct experience, observed practice, or informed hypothesis, and to say which year they were last active in the role.
- Seek disconfirmation. For every statement ask what is wrong before asking what is right. "Accurate as written" is an acceptable answer only after the respondent has tried to break it.
- Force ranking and single choices. Do not accept "all of the above". If a respondent cannot choose, record that as a finding about the list, not as an answer.
- Do not defend or re-explain the model when it is challenged. Record the challenge verbatim.
- Capture disagreement between respondents. Do not resolve it through facilitator inference.
- Proxy responses can reject, edit or fail to break a statement. They cannot confirm it. Confirmation requires current bankers under the charter's two-participant rule.

## Expected Validation Output

The proxy round should produce, for the Coverage model as it stands:

1. A disposition for each of the five JTBD statements: survives unchallenged, needs edit (with the edit), wrong altitude, or not recognised.
2. A verdict on each lens boundary, naming any pair that collides in practice.
3. Any standing senior job the five lenses miss, with an example of that work.
4. A verdict on the actions and commitments lens: standing lens, execution layer, both, or neither.
5. Each lens's scenarios ranked by frequency and by consequence, plus missing scenarios and any scenario that is really a different use case.
6. The actor split per lens: what is irreducibly MD work, what is team work, what is product-partner work.
7. At least one concrete episode per respondent, mapped onto the model, with the points where the model fails to describe what happened.
8. Observed current practice per lens, separate from desired practice.
9. Value ranges in units for each of the four business outcomes.
10. A language verdict: which words a banker would not use, with substitutions.
11. No more than five focused questions for current bankers, including at least one episode request.

## MV00 - The Episode First

**Question:** Think of a recent consequential situation from your time in Coverage. A signal you acted on or missed, a relationship that needed senior attention, an opportunity that was lost, parked or won late, or a meeting that changed something. Walk me through it from the moment it first became visible to the point where it was resolved or went quiet.

**Response mode:** Open narrative, ten to fifteen minutes, before any statement is shown. Interviewer records the timeline, the people, the artefacts and the decisions.

**Probes:**

- How did it first become visible, and to whom?
- What did you rely on at each step: a tracker, email, a call, a meeting, memory, a system?
- Who owned the next move at each step, and did they know they owned it?
- Where did the chain break, slow down or recover?
- What was the consequence for the client, the relationship, the franchise or the revenue?
- What would have had to be visible earlier to change the outcome?
- Was anything deliberately not done, and was that the right call?

## MV01 - Map The Episode Onto The Model

**Question:** Now look at the pre-read pack. Which of the five lenses did your episode pass through, in what order, and where does the model describe what happened well, poorly, or not at all?

**Response mode:** Tag each lens touched (intelligence, relationships, pipeline, meetings, actions and commitments). For the lens where the episode spent most time, walk the six use-case steps and mark each as happened as described, happened differently, or did not happen.

**Possible answers for each step:**

- **Happened as described** - The step occurred in substance, whatever the tooling.
- **Happened differently** - Describe the difference in who, what evidence, or what judgment.
- **Did not happen** - And say whether its absence mattered.
- **Happened but belongs to a different lens** - Say which.

## MV02 - Test Each Job Statement

**Question:** For each of the five job statements in the pre-read pack, try to break it. Then choose one disposition per statement.

**Response mode:** One disposition per statement, with a reason. Where the disposition is an edit, give the edited wording.

**Possible answers:**

- **Survives** - You tried to break it and could not. Say what you tried.
- **Right job, wrong wording** - Give the words you would change and why.
- **Right wording, wrong altitude** - Too detailed, too abstract, or too system-shaped. Say which.
- **Missing or wrong trade-off** - The "without" clause does not match what senior Coverage actually guards against.
- **Not a job I recognise** - Senior Coverage does not do this as a distinct job. Say where the work actually sits.
- **Two jobs in one** - Say where it splits.

## MV03 - Lens Boundaries

**Question:** The model keeps five lenses distinct and routes any situation that touches several to the accountable owner. In your experience, which pairs collide so often that the boundary is a fiction, and which boundary is drawn in the wrong place?

**Response mode:** Rank up to two collisions. For each, say what work falls between the lenses today.

**Possible answers:**

- Intelligence and relationships: interpreting a signal and deciding to engage are one act.
- Relationships and meetings: the enduring relationship job and the fixed-window meeting job are not separable.
- Pipeline and actions: opportunity trajectory and follow-through are the same thing in practice.
- Meetings and actions: the meeting is not over until follow-through is done, so they are one job.
- Intelligence and pipeline: most signals are opportunity signals, so triage is pipeline work.
- Umbrella and everything: the umbrella statement is where the real job lives and the lenses are artificial.
- The boundaries hold.
- Another collision.

## MV04 - The Missing Job

**Question:** Think about the senior Coverage work you did in a typical quarter. What did you spend material time on that none of the five lenses describes?

**Response mode:** Name the work first, with an example. Then say whether it deserves a distinct job, is context inside a lens, or is a triggered guardrail.

**Possible answers:**

- Franchise and sector strategy: portfolio and competitive positioning.
- People leadership: staffing, coaching, team health.
- Risk and controls as standing work rather than triggered.
- Internal stakeholder management beyond orchestrating the bank around a client.
- Commercial performance: revenue, wallet, outlook beyond pipeline.
- Capacity: allocating scarce team and specialist time.
- Nothing material is missing.
- Another job.

## MV05 - Actions And Commitments

**Question:** Every lens in the model hands its output to an "actions and commitments" lens that is only a working hypothesis. In practice, who owns follow-through once a senior has decided, and does that work deserve its own standing senior review?

**Response mode:** Choose one architecture and name the real owner of follow-through in your experience.

**Possible answers:**

- **A standing lens** - Seniors review consequential commitments and blockers across all domains as a distinct job.
- **An execution layer beneath each lens** - Follow-through belongs to whichever lens generated it; there is no separate senior job.
- **Both** - Each lens owns its actions and a senior view exposes cross-domain commitments and blockers.
- **An exception view only** - Follow-through surfaces to the senior only when impact, timing or blockage crosses a threshold.
- **It should not exist as a lens** - The accountable owner's own lens is the destination; "actions" is a tooling idea, not a job.
- Another model.

## MV06 - Scenario Ranking

**Question:** For each lens, rank its scenarios by how often they occurred in your experience and by how much was at stake when they did. Then name one scenario that is missing and one scenario that is really a different piece of work.

**Response mode:** Two rankings per lens (frequency, consequence). One missing scenario per lens or "none". One misfiled scenario per lens or "none".

**Scenarios to rank:**

- Intelligence: short-window consequential signal (A), uncertain high-impact signal (B), deliberate non-action or monitoring (C).
- Relationships: institution relationship review (A), time-sensitive risk or commitment (B), tier-based cadence review (C).
- Pipeline: recurring pipeline session (A), material change or closing window (B), parked reactivation (C), ambiguous early idea (D), restricted cross-GIB opportunity (E).
- Meetings: forming client decision (A), relationship-sensitive listening or repair (B), major commitment or cross-JPM meeting (C).

**Probe:** Does any scenario produce something different from its parent use case's value delivered? If so it is a use case, not a scenario. Say which.

## MV07 - The Actor Split

**Question:** For each lens, what is irreducibly the MD's own work, what does the VP or team do, and what do product or regional partners do? The model leaves every contributor role marked as unvalidated.

**Response mode:** Per lens, three short lists. Then answer the probe.

**Possible MD-only work (choose the ones that are truly irreducible):**

- Judging whether something matters in context.
- Judging whether evidence is credible enough for the contemplated use.
- Choosing the intended client outcome.
- Deciding whether and how to use relationship capital.
- Orchestrating the bank around the institution and resolving ownership.
- Setting and enforcing the senior-ready bar.
- Deciding to reshape, park or stop.
- Deciding to do nothing.

**Probe:** In your experience, which of these did a strong VP actually do without the MD, and did that work?

## MV08 - How It Is Actually Done Today

**Question:** Leaving aside how it should work, how was each lens's work actually done when you were last in the role? What held the state, who kept it current, and which parts of the model's six steps simply never happened?

**Response mode:** Per lens, name the artefacts and the keeper. Label every answer observed practice or direct experience. Hypotheses are not accepted for this question.

**Possible state holders:**

- CRM or deal system of record.
- Personal or team tracker (spreadsheet, document).
- Email and calendar.
- Meeting notes, where they existed.
- Pitch and deal materials.
- Junior banker memory.
- MD memory.
- Nowhere; it was reconstructed on demand.

**Probes:** How much senior time went into reconstruction before a review? Which step in the model would have felt like administration rather than work?

## MV09 - Value In Units

**Question:** The model's four business outcomes have no baselines. From your experience, give a range for each of the following. A wide range is fine. A refusal to estimate is recorded as a finding.

**Response mode:** Ranges with the experience label. One line each.

**Measures:**

- Senior hours per week spent reconstructing status, context or evidence rather than exercising judgment.
- Consequential client or market events per quarter that reached you after the useful response window had passed.
- Share of the reported pipeline you believed was stale or not credible at any given review.
- Priority client institutions per year that went a quarter without purposeful senior contact.
- Viable ideas per year that were never recognised as opportunities until a competitor or the client made them visible.
- What one missed window cost, in whatever unit you would have used at the time: fee, mandate, relationship standing, or senior credibility.
- Consequential meetings per quarter that produced no owned follow-through within a week.

## MV10 - Language

**Question:** Read the five job statements and one business use case aloud as if to a peer. Which words would a senior Coverage banker not use, and what would they say instead?

**Response mode:** List each word or phrase and the substitute. Then rate the overall readability.

**Candidate terms to test:** intelligence, disposition, destination lens, stewardship, evidential strength, purposeful next movement, accepted judgment, consequential, orchestrate, umbrella intent, value delivered.

**Possible readability verdicts:**

- A banker would read this and recognise their work.
- A banker would recognise the work but not the words.
- A banker would stop reading.

## MV11 - The Model As A Litmus Test

**Question:** This model is meant to be the test a product team must pass to show it adds value to Coverage. What would make it useless for that purpose, and how could a team claim alignment to it without actually adding value?

**Response mode:** Open answer, then rank the strongest two failure modes.

**Possible failure modes:**

- Statements are broad enough that anything can claim to serve them.
- No outcome has a number, so nothing can be shown to move.
- The model describes desired progress, so a team can serve the aspiration while making the real work harder.
- Non-action is a valid result, so a team can claim value from a product nobody uses.
- The model reflects one MD, so a team can pick the parts that suit it.
- The language is abstract enough that bankers will not challenge a claim made in it.
- The missing actions lens lets any follow-through tooling claim alignment by default.
- Another failure mode.

## Appendix A - Pre-Read Pack

Send this to respondents ahead of the session. Everything here is a hypothesis for the purposes of the interview.

### The five job statements

**Intelligence triage.** When new information may affect my clients or franchise, help me isolate consequential change, understand its client-specific implications and evidential strength, and choose the appropriate response and intended outcome, so we act early when it matters, confidently do nothing when it does not, and avoid surprises without consuming senior attention on noise.

**Client relationship management.** When managing a priority client institution, help me judge and strengthen the quality of the relationship using meaningful markers of trust, access, reciprocity, and engagement, informed by what we understand of the client's evolving agenda, so JPM remains relevant, trusted, and positioned to influence and compete.

**Opportunity pipeline stewardship.** When managing opportunities from idea through close, help me maintain disciplined stewardship through clear accountability, purposeful movement, regular challenge, and deliberate parking or exit, so the franchise can direct effort and protect its outlook without losing viable opportunities or creating administrative burden, even when client intent is ambiguous and judgments differ.

**Consequential client meetings.** When a consequential client interaction creates a fixed window to learn or influence, help me enter with a grounded hypothesis, timely context, aligned JPM posture, and critical uncertainty resolved, so the meeting clarifies intent, strengthens trust, and advances, protects, or deliberately defers a decision, with the changed state carried into owned follow-through.

**Umbrella.** When client and franchise conditions are changing, help me judge what is consequential in context and maintain momentum toward the outcomes that matter, so JPM responds coherently while there is still time to influence the result, without confusing activity with progress.

**Working hypothesis, actions and commitments.** When consequential outcomes depend on coordinated work, help me translate intent into an accepted owner and explicit next commitment, and keep that commitment connected to visible outcome progress, so responsibilities resolve clearly and owners can move independently without work drifting or repeatedly returning to me.

### The lens boundaries

- Intelligence decides how consequential evidence should be interpreted and routed.
- Relationships judge and strengthen institution-level relationship quality.
- Pipeline governs disciplined opportunity stewardship from idea through close.
- Actions and commitments translate intent into accepted ownership, explicit next commitments, and visible outcome progress.
- Meetings prepare for and convert fixed client-interaction windows into interpreted, routed, and initiated progress.
- Capacity is deferred. Risk and controls appear only when triggered.
- When a situation touches several lenses, the accountable owner's lens leads and the others are linked.

### The six steps of each business use case

| Lens | Steps |
| --- | --- |
| Intelligence | Reduce noise; identify affected clients and context; explain consequence and evidence; judge the response; record the disposition; route to the destination lens. |
| Relationships | Establish institution change and trajectory; interpret relationship markers; judge whether senior attention is warranted; set objective and stakeholder; choose the engagement disposition; review movement and update judgment. |
| Pipeline | Recognise an opportunity or review trigger; assemble evidence and views; challenge maturity and trajectory; choose the stewardship response; confirm accountable stewardship and next movement; update the shared picture and review conditions. |
| Meetings | Establish why this interaction matters now; form the hypothesis and intended outcome; align JPM posture and resolve critical uncertainty; engage and adapt; interpret the outcome; make commitments explicit, update lenses, initiate follow-through. |

### The fourteen scenarios

Intelligence A short-window signal, B uncertain high-impact signal, C deliberate non-action. Relationships A institution review, B time-sensitive risk or commitment, C tier-based cadence. Pipeline A recurring session, B material change or closing window, C parked reactivation, D ambiguous early idea, E restricted cross-GIB. Meetings A forming client decision, B listening or repair, C major commitment or cross-JPM.

### The actor split as currently recorded

Senior Coverage MD: judges materiality, credibility, response, intended client outcome, relationship quality, senior attention, pursuit, effort, reshape or stop. Coverage opportunity team: maintains evidence, stewards opportunities, executes next movement. All other contributor roles: unvalidated.

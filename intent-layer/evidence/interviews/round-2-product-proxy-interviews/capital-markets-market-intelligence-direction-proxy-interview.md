# Capital Markets Market Intelligence Direction - Proxy Interview

## Interview Record

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Interview ID** | `INT-CM-MI-001` |
| **Question range** | CMI01-CMI12 |
| **Status** | `Draft` |

## Interviewer Guidance

- Share all twelve questions in advance.
- For every selected answer, tag it Common, ECM, DCM, or Desk-specific.
- Ask respondents to distinguish direct experience, observed practice, and informed hypothesis.
- Force prioritization: broad relevance does not make every item part of the primary job.
- Preserve differences among ECM, investment-grade DCM, leveraged finance, and syndicate when they materially change the decision.
- Proxy responses establish candidate direction only. They do not confirm current banker intent.

## Expected Direction-Setting Output

The proxy round should produce:

1. A candidate shared Capital Markets market-intelligence job.
2. The key issuer and transaction decisions intelligence must change.
3. A common intelligence core plus explicit ECM/DCM variations.
4. Minimum issuer-translation, trust, and uncertainty contracts.
5. A candidate production, authority, and workflow-routing model.
6. Leading measures, lagging outcomes, and control guardrails.
7. A focused current-banker validation plan split only where variations warrant it.

## CMI01 - Primary Progress

**Question:** What should excellent market intelligence enable a senior Capital Markets banker to do more reliably than today?

**Response mode:** Select and rank up to three primary outcomes. Tag each Common, ECM, DCM, or Desk-specific.

**Possible answers:**

- **Detect actionable market windows** - Recognize when conditions support or constrain issuer action.
- **Translate markets into issuer-specific advice** - Explain what changed, why it matters to this issuer, and what to consider next.
- **Identify origination opportunities earlier** - Connect market change to capital needs, readiness, or transaction paths.
- **Shape financing or equity strategy** - Compare product, structure, timing, and execution alternatives.
- **Prepare differentiated client engagement** - Bring a clear thesis rather than generic market updates.
- **Prioritize issuer attention** - Focus senior time where market change and issuer readiness intersect.
- **Protect transaction execution** - Anticipate pricing, demand, supply, ratings, valuation, or timing risk.
- **Avoid consequential market surprises** - Reduce late awareness of conditions that should have changed advice or execution.
- **Something else** - Describe the missing progress.

## CMI02 - Decisions Changed

**Question:** Which Capital Markets decisions should market intelligence materially change rather than merely inform?

**Response mode:** Select and rank the three most consequential; tag each by cohort.

**Possible answers:**

- Whether and when to approach an issuer.
- Whether to recommend proceeding, accelerating, waiting, or stopping.
- Which product, instrument, or transaction path to propose.
- Size, tenor, maturity, tranche, or structural choices.
- Valuation, pricing, spread, discount, or guidance range.
- Investor targeting, positioning, allocation, or distribution strategy.
- Launch timing and execution-window judgment.
- Underwriting, balance-sheet, or execution-risk posture.
- Which issuers or live transactions deserve senior attention.
- Whether deliberate monitoring or non-action is appropriate.
- Another decision.

## CMI04 - Relevant Time Horizons

**Question:** At which horizons does market intelligence create a distinct Capital Markets decision?

**Response mode:** Select each material horizon, identify the decision it supports, and tag cohort variation.

**Possible answers:**

- **Live or intraday** - Price movement, book dynamics, volatility, or execution risk.
- **Next 24-72 hours** - Launch, pricing, investor engagement, or event response.
- **One to four weeks** - Near-term issuance window, competing supply, or client preparation.
- **Quarter or funding calendar** - Planned issuance, refinancing, readiness, or capital action.
- **12-24 months** - Capital structure, maturity wall, IPO readiness, sponsor monetization, or strategic financing plan.
- **Structural or multiyear** - Persistent market, regulatory, sector, capital, or investor-base change.

## CMI05 - Materiality Test

**Question:** What makes a market development consequential enough to surface to a senior Capital Markets banker?

**Response mode:** Select and rank the strongest tests; state whether several tests must combine.

**Possible answers:**

- It changes an issuer's capital need or readiness.
- It opens, narrows, or closes an execution window.
- It materially changes pricing, spread, discount, or valuation support.
- It changes execution probability or risk.
- It changes investor appetite, distribution, or likely book quality.
- It creates meaningful competing supply or peer pressure.
- It changes ratings, liquidity, maturity, or capital-structure considerations.
- It changes a client decision or creates a reason to engage now.
- It affects several relevant issuers or a desk portfolio.
- Delay would be costly or difficult to reverse.
- Another materiality test.

## CMI06 - Issuer Translation

**Question:** What must be added to a market fact before it becomes useful Capital Markets intelligence for a specific issuer or transaction?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum required elements and tag material ECM/DCM differences.

**Possible answers:**

- The affected issuer, sector, or transaction.
- Why the development matters to that issuer now.
- Issuer readiness, capital need, maturity, ownership, or strategic context.
- Product, structure, or transaction alternatives implicated.
- Evidence, provenance, freshness, and confidence.
- Expected window duration and relevant triggers.
- Comparable issuance, trading, pricing, valuation, or aftermarket evidence.
- Investor-demand or syndicate interpretation.
- A client conversation thesis and likely questions or objections.
- Recommended response, owner, or watch condition.
- Another required element.

## CMI07 - Evidence And Trust

**Question:** What evidence is sufficient for a banker to use market intelligence in client advice, pricing, or execution judgment?

**Response mode:** Define the minimum trust contract and where standards rise with consequence.

**Possible answers:**

- Named authoritative source and visible timestamp.
- Clear mapping from market evidence to the issuer or transaction.
- Provenance from source fact to interpretation or recommendation.
- Methodology, assumptions, and comparable-set definition.
- Freshness appropriate to the decision window.
- Explicit confidence and known unknowns.
- Clear distinction between fact, model output, desk color, and judgment.
- Alternative interpretations or dissenting market views.
- Named human owner or accountable reviewer.
- Sensitivity, wall-cross, entitlement, and permitted-use classification.
- Another trust requirement.

## CMI08 - Uncertainty And Scenarios

**Question:** How should consequential but uncertain market intelligence be handled in origination, client advice, or execution?

**Response mode:** Choose the governing model and identify where ECM/DCM or lifecycle stage changes it.

**Possible answers:**

- Present a range of scenarios with explicit triggers.
- Surface as a hypothesis with confidence and evidence gaps.
- Corroborate before using in client advice.
- Route to syndicate, research, trading, ratings, or another SME first.
- Use as a client question rather than a recommendation.
- Monitor for a defined confirmation or invalidation condition.
- Vary treatment by impact, reversibility, and remaining execution window.
- Another model.

## CMI12 - Success And Failure

**Question:** What would prove Capital Markets intelligence is improving the business, and what failure must it avoid?

**Response mode:** Select one or two leading measures, one or two lagging outcomes, and the most important guardrails. Tag cohort variation.

**Possible success measures:**

- Earlier issuer engagement around a relevant market window.
- Better issuer-specific advice and client conversations.
- Earlier or higher-quality origination opportunities.
- Better timing, structure, pricing, valuation, or execution decisions.
- Improved mandate conversion or competitive positioning.
- Fewer avoidable market or execution surprises.
- Less banker time spent finding, reconciling, and repackaging information.
- Better capture and reuse of investor feedback and transaction outcomes.
- More appropriate recommendations to wait or take no action.

**Possible failure modes:**

- Excessive noise or undifferentiated alerts.
- Generic market commentary without issuer relevance.
- Stale data or hidden assumptions.
- False precision or unsupported timing, pricing, or valuation recommendations.
- Incorrect issuer or transaction mapping.
- Conflicting analysis across desks without accountable resolution.
- Leakage of restricted, wall-cross, client-sensitive, or internal desk information.
- Activity and dashboard consumption without a changed decision or client outcome.

## Proxy Synthesis And Banker Validation Gate

After proxy interviews, produce:

- the proposed shared Capital Markets job;
- common versus ECM/DCM/desk-specific decision and information needs;
- proposed scope and exclusions;
- minimum issuer-translation, trust, and uncertainty contracts;
- candidate ownership and workflow-routing model;
- selected measures and guardrails;
- proxy disagreements and evidence gaps; and
- separate focused banker-validation sets only where material cohort differences remain.

Current bankers should be asked to correct the candidate model, supply concrete episodes, define consequential thresholds and authority, and validate final job wording. They should not be asked to repeat the full directional questionnaire unless proxy findings prove unreliable.

# Coverage Market Intelligence Direction - Proxy Interview

## Interview Record

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Interview ID** | `INT-COV-MI-001` |
| **Question range** | MI01-MI12 |
| **Status** | `Draft` |
| **Direction-setting audience** | Former senior Coverage bankers and market-intelligence SMEs |
| **Focused validation audience** | Current Senior Coverage MDs after proxy synthesis |
| **Purpose** | Establish the likely market-intelligence direction, boundaries, ownership model, and success tests without using current banker time for broad discovery. |
| **Existing foundation** | The confirmed Coverage intelligence-triage JTBD establishes materiality, client implication, evidential strength, response, and intended outcome. This interview narrows that job to market intelligence and its operating model. |

## Interviewer Guidance

- Share all twelve questions in advance.
- Ask respondents to label each answer as direct experience, observed practice, or informed hypothesis.
- Seek the strongest two or three choices rather than accepting every option.
- Capture material disagreements; do not resolve them through facilitator inference.
- Ask for examples where useful, but reserve detailed episode reconstruction and final wording for focused banker validation.
- Proxy responses establish candidate direction only. They do not confirm banker intent.

## Expected Direction-Setting Output

The proxy round should produce:

1. The primary progress market intelligence should create for Coverage.
2. The banker decisions it should materially change.
3. Initial market-domain and time-horizon boundaries.
4. A minimum client-translation and evidence contract.
5. A candidate production, ownership, and workflow-routing model.
6. Two or three success measures plus unacceptable failure modes.
7. A short list of disputed assumptions for current-bank validation.

## MI01 - Primary Progress

**Question:** What should excellent market intelligence enable a senior Coverage banker to do more reliably than today?

**Response mode:** Select and rank up to three primary outcomes. Classify other selected items as enablers or downstream benefits.

**Possible answers:**

- **Anticipate client needs** - Recognize likely client concerns or decisions before they are explicitly stated.
- **Improve client advice** - Bring timely, differentiated perspective rather than generic market commentary.
- **Identify opportunities earlier** - Connect market change to plausible client needs or transaction paths.
- **Challenge assumptions** - Test existing client, relationship, or opportunity judgments against changed conditions.
- **Time engagement better** - Recognize when a useful client-intervention window opens or closes.
- **Manage client or franchise risk** - Detect consequences that require protection, preparation, or deliberate non-action.
- **Allocate senior attention** - Focus interpretation and engagement on the most consequential changes.
- **Avoid consequential surprises** - Reduce late awareness of conditions the banker should have anticipated.
- **Something else** - Describe the missing progress.

## MI02 - Decisions Changed

**Question:** Which banker decisions should market intelligence materially change rather than merely inform?

**Response mode:** Select the consequential decisions and rank the three most important.

**Possible answers:**

- Who to engage.
- When to engage.
- What conversation thesis, question, or perspective to bring.
- What advice or recommendation to give.
- Whether an opportunity judgment has changed.
- Whether transaction timing or readiness has changed.
- Whether valuation, financing, or structure alternatives should be reconsidered.
- Whether client or franchise risk requires action.
- Where senior or team attention should move.
- Whether deliberate monitoring or non-action is appropriate.
- Another decision.

## MI03 - Intelligence Scope

**Question:** Which market-intelligence domains belong in the first scope for senior Coverage, and which should remain specialist context available on demand?

**Response mode:** Rank up to five primary domains; mark the remainder as secondary, specialist-only, or out of scope.

**Possible answers:**

- Macro conditions and economic outlook.
- Rates, credit, liquidity, and financing conditions.
- Equity markets and sector performance.
- Sector structure and operating trends.
- Capital-markets issuance and transaction activity.
- Investor sentiment, flows, and demand.
- Valuation and comparable-company movement.
- Competitor strategy and relevant peer actions.
- Regulation, policy, and legal developments.
- Geopolitical and country risk.
- Client-specific market exposure or vulnerability.
- Another domain.

## MI04 - Relevant Time Horizons

**Question:** At which time horizons does market intelligence create a distinct Coverage decision or client-engagement need?

**Response mode:** Select each material horizon and state the distinct decision it supports.

**Possible answers:**

- **Intraday** - Immediate market dislocation or client-sensitive event.
- **Days** - A near-term engagement, event, or decision window.
- **Weeks** - Developing market thesis or client agenda implication.
- **Quarter** - Planning, financing, strategic, or portfolio decisions.
- **12-24 months** - Anticipated client needs and medium-term strategic positioning.
- **Structural or multiyear** - Enduring sector, policy, technology, or business-model change.

## MI05 - Materiality Test

**Question:** What makes a market development consequential enough to surface to a senior Coverage banker?

**Response mode:** Select and rank the strongest tests. Explain whether several tests must combine.

**Possible answers:**

- Material client exposure.
- A closing or opening decision window.
- Meaningful financial or valuation impact.
- Strategic relevance to the client's agenda.
- A relationship opportunity or threat.
- A transaction, financing, or capital-allocation implication.
- Significant deviation from prior expectations.
- Consequence across several covered clients or the franchise.
- Costly or irreversible delay.
- Another materiality test.

## MI06 - Client Translation

**Question:** What must be added to a market fact before it becomes useful Coverage intelligence?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum required elements; identify what may remain optional or progressive disclosure.

**Possible answers:**

- The affected client or client segment.
- The client-specific implication.
- Why the change matters now.
- Evidence, provenance, freshness, and confidence.
- Expected duration or decision window.
- Comparable precedents or peer context.
- JPM interpretation or differentiated perspective.
- A potential conversation thesis or question.
- Opportunity, relationship, or risk implication.
- Recommended response, owner, or watch condition.
- Another required element.

## MI07 - Evidence And Trust

**Question:** What evidence is sufficient for a banker to use market intelligence in judgment or a client conversation?

**Response mode:** Define the minimum trust contract and where standards should rise with consequence.

**Possible answers:**

- Named and authoritative source.
- Visible timestamp and freshness.
- Provenance from source fact to interpretation.
- Corroboration from independent evidence.
- Clear distinction between fact, inference, and opinion.
- Explicit confidence and known unknowns.
- Methodology or assumptions where analysis is derived.
- Dissenting or alternative interpretations.
- Sensitivity and permissioning classification.
- Named human owner or accountable reviewer.
- Another trust requirement.

## MI08 - Uncertainty

**Question:** How should high-impact but uncertain market intelligence be handled?

**Response mode:** Choose the closest governing model and note exceptions.

**Possible answers:**

- Elevate promptly as an explicit hypothesis.
- Investigate or corroborate before surfacing.
- Show directly with uncertainty and source limitations exposed.
- Route first to the relevant market or sector SME.
- Monitor for a defined corroboration condition.
- Use as a question or scenario, not a recommendation.
- Vary treatment according to impact, reversibility, and decision-window length.
- Another model.

**Possible answers:**

- A concise material-change summary.
- A client-impact view.
- A market or sector thesis.
- A scenario range with triggers.
- A client-conversation brief.
- An opportunity hypothesis.
- A risk or watch condition.
- A recommended action or deliberate non-action.
- Links to the supporting evidence and dissenting views.
- Another output.

- Another model.

## MI11 - Workflow Destination

**Question:** Once market intelligence has been interpreted, where should it go so it changes work rather than becoming another feed?

**Response mode:** Select all legitimate destinations and define the routing condition for each.

**Possible answers:**

- Client-institution or relationship plan.
- Opportunity pipeline.
- Meeting preparation.
- Action or commitment.
- Temporary cross-bank situation view.
- Market or client watchlist.
- Deliberate no-action or monitoring record.
- Reusable institutional knowledge.
- Specialist review queue.
- Another destination.

## MI12 - Success And Failure

**Question:** What would prove the market-intelligence model is improving the business, and what failure must it avoid?

**Response mode:** Select one or two leading measures, one or two lagging outcomes, and the most important guardrails.

**Possible success measures:**

- Earlier relevant client engagement.
- Better client conversations or advice.
- Earlier recognition of opportunities or risks.
- Faster evidence-to-decision movement.
- Fewer consequential surprises.
- Less banker time spent finding and reconciling information.
- More appropriate deliberate non-action.
- Improved commercial outcomes attributable to earlier or better judgment.

**Possible failure modes:**

- Excessive noise or alert fatigue.
- Generic content without client relevance.
- False confidence or unsupported recommendations.
- Stale intelligence.
- Duplicated analysis across teams.
- Missed consequential signals.
- Restricted-information leakage.
- Activity without a changed decision or client outcome.

## Proxy Synthesis And Banker Validation Gate

After proxy interviews, create a candidate direction containing:

- top progress and decisions;
- proposed scope and exclusions;
- minimum client-translation and trust contracts;
- candidate ownership and routing model;
- selected measures and guardrails;
- proxy disagreements and evidence gaps; and
- no more than five focused questions for current bankers, including at least one concrete episode.

## CACT01 - Primary Progress

**Question:** What should excellent management of actions and commitments enable a Senior Coverage MD and team to do more reliably?

**Response mode:** Select and rank up to three primary outcomes; classify the remainder as enablers or downstream benefits.

**Possible answers:**

- Translate intent into accepted ownership and explicit next commitment.
- Preserve strategic intent through delegation.
- Keep commitments connected to visible client or franchise progress.
- Reveal where MD judgment or action is blocking movement.
- Resolve dependencies before consequences compound.
- Enable owners to move without repeated reinterpretation.
- Fulfil or consciously renegotiate consequential promises.
- Stop low-value work sooner.
- Release senior attention at the right point.
- Another outcome.

## CACT03 - Entry Threshold

**Response mode:** Select and rank the strongest tests; define any exception-only model.

**Possible answers:**

- Material blocker or dependency.

## CACT05 - Accepted Ownership

**Question:** What proves that ownership is real rather than nominal, and how should ownership conflicts be resolved?

**Response mode:** Select the acceptance signals and choose the conflict-resolution model.

**Possible answers:**

- Owner has authority, capacity, and access to required inputs.
- Owner can explain the quality bar and escalation rule.
- Dependencies have named contributors.
- Owner may renegotiate openly when assumptions change.
- One accountable owner remains clear even when work is shared.
- Relevant business head resolves cross-team ownership conflict.
- Ownership changes explicitly rather than drifting silently.
- Another rule.

## CACT09 - Standing Lens Or Embedded Layer

**Question:** How should Actions relate to Intelligence, Relationships, Pipeline, and Meetings?

**Response mode:** Choose the closest architecture and identify what would falsify it.

**Possible answers:**

- A standing lens for consequential commitments and blockers across domains.
- An execution layer beneath every lens with no independent view.
- Both: each domain owns its work while a standing view exposes cross-domain commitments and MD blockers.
- An exception view shown only when consequence, timing, or blockage crosses a threshold.
- A personal work queue separate from shared commitment management.

## CACT11 - Automation, Visibility, And Controls

**Question:** What should be inferred or automated, what requires explicit human contribution, and what must never occur without approval?

**Response mode:** Classify each behavior as infer, suggest, require confirmation, or prohibit.

**Possible answers:**

- Infer routine status and activity from trusted systems.
- Suggest likely commitments, owners, dependencies, or slippage.
- Require human confirmation of intended outcome and accepted owner.
- Draft updates or follow-up communications for review.
- Escalate when an agreed threshold is crossed.
- Auto-close work from evidence of completion.
- Auto-reassign ownership.
- Auto-send client or internal commitments.
- Preserve provenance, audit history, sensitivity, and entitlements.
- Another behavior.

## CACT12 - Success, Failure, And Representative Episode

**Question:** What would prove Actions and Commitments is creating outcome velocity rather than administrative control? Ground the answer in one commitment that advanced, drifted, or repeatedly returned to the MD.

**Response mode:** Select the strongest success and failure evidence, then walk through a direct example where possible.

**Possible success measures:**

- Commitments resolve explicitly.
- Client or franchise outcomes advance.
- Owners move independently without repeated reinterpretation.
- Blockers resolve before consequences compound.
- Senior attention is released appropriately.
- Low-value work stops sooner.
- Less time is spent chasing and reconstructing status.

**Possible failure modes:**

- Task completion without outcome progress.
- Named but unaccepted ownership.
- Hidden MD bottlenecks.
- Delegation losing strategic intent.
- Excessive reminders and status administration.
- Silent slippage until the intervention window closes.
- Cross-domain commitments with conflicting owners.

**Episode probes:** What was promised, who accepted it, what context survived delegation, what changed, when should intervention have occurred, and what evidence finally closed or redirected the commitment?

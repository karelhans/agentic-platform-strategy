## Interviewer Guidance

- Share all twelve questions in advance.
- Ask respondents to label answers as direct experience, observed practice, or informed hypothesis.
- Focus on consequential client interactions, not routine scheduling, hosting, note taking, or one meeting technology.
- Keep enduring relationship stewardship, opportunity stewardship, and detailed action execution in their separate interviews.
- Preserve differences among decision-forming, relationship-sensitive, and major cross-JPM meetings.
- Proxy responses establish candidate direction only. They do not reconfirm banker intent.

## Expected Direction-Setting Output

The proxy round should produce:

1. The primary progress and threshold for a consequential meeting.
2. A minimum readiness, hypothesis, posture, and uncertainty contract.
3. In-meeting adaptation and changed-state interpretation rules.
4. Completion, commitment, handoff, and contributor boundaries.
5. Scenario variations, measures, guardrails, and episode evidence.
6. A short current-banker validation set.

## CMEET01 - Primary Progress

**Question:** What should a consequential client meeting enable a Senior Coverage MD and JPM team to create or protect?

**Response mode:** Select and rank up to three primary outcomes; classify other selected items as enablers or downstream benefits.

**Possible answers:**

- Learn material client intent.
- Improve trust, candor, or relationship quality.
- Influence or protect a forming client decision.
- Advance a relationship or opportunity outcome.
- Align JPM around one coherent client posture.
- Make a consequential commitment explicit.
- Validate deliberate non-action or deferral.
- Recognize that a prepared hypothesis is wrong.
- Another outcome.

## CMEET02 - Consequential Meeting Threshold

**Question:** What makes a client interaction worthy of a distinct consequential-meeting process and Senior Coverage MD attention?

**Response mode:** Select and rank the strongest tests; distinguish required from sufficient conditions.

**Possible answers:**

- Relationship leverage where MD presence can alter the result.
- A forming or time-sensitive client decision.
- Material relationship risk or repair need.
- A major client or JPM commitment.
- Cross-JPM orchestration or conflicting positions.
- Protocol, seniority, or right-to-engage requirements.
- Strategic ambiguity that requires contextual senior judgment.
- A fixed window to learn or influence.
- Another threshold.

## CMEET03 - Minimum Readiness

**Question:** What must be understood before entering a consequential client interaction?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum readiness elements and identify what may remain on demand.

**Possible answers:**

- Why the moment matters now.
- Relevant client, relationship, and stakeholder context.
- Current opportunity, commitment, and prior-interaction state.
- A grounded client hypothesis.
- Intended learning, influence, protection, repair, or decision outcome.
- Material evidence and uncertainty.
- JPM posture, participants, and roles.
- Sensitivities, controls, and what must be avoided.
- Likely client questions or objections.
- Another readiness element.

## CMEET04 - Hypothesis And Intended Outcome

**Question:** How should the meeting hypothesis and intended outcome be framed so preparation remains useful without scripting the interaction?

**Response mode:** Choose the governing model and identify who challenges or accepts it.

**Possible answers:**

- One primary hypothesis with explicit evidence and uncertainty.
- Several plausible hypotheses ranked by consequence.
- A learning objective where influence would be premature.
- A specific decision or commitment objective.
- A relationship objective such as trust, access, or repair.
- An opportunity objective such as qualification or next client movement.
- A deliberate non-action or deferral test.
- Adaptive by meeting type and client context.
- Another model.

## CMEET05 - JPM Posture And Participants

**Question:** How should JPM align message, questions, participants, and decision rights before the meeting?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum alignment contract and identify who resolves disagreement.

**Possible answers:**

- One accepted meeting purpose and intended client outcome.
- Named lead and participant roles.
- Agreed facts, hypotheses, and areas of uncertainty.
- Clear division between listening, advising, and asking.
- Consistent JPM position where consistency is required.
- Explicit areas where product or regional views may differ.
- Decision rights for commitments made in the room.
- Protocol, sequencing, and escalation for sensitive issues.
- Another element.

## CMEET06 - Critical Uncertainty And Controls

**Question:** Which unresolved issues should cause the team to proceed, narrow the purpose, change participants, or defer the interaction?

**Response mode:** Classify each issue as acceptable uncertainty, must-resolve, or stop condition.

**Possible answers:**

- Unverified fact that could materially change advice.
- Unclear client decision-maker or authority.
- Conflicting JPM positions.
- Relationship or protocol sensitivity.
- Restricted, wall-cross, legal, or conduct issue.
- Unknown commitment authority.
- Unclear purpose or no credible right to engage.
- Missing evidence that can instead be handled as a client question.
- Another issue.

## CMEET07 - In-Meeting Adaptation

**Question:** What should the Senior Coverage MD and team be prepared to notice and adapt to during the interaction?

**Response mode:** Select the consequential signals and define who may change posture or make commitments.

**Possible answers:**

- New or contradicted client intent.
- Changes in candor, trust, or relationship risk.
- A forming decision or altered decision window.
- New stakeholder influence or resistance.
- Evidence that invalidates the prepared hypothesis.
- Opportunity, timing, or competitive change.
- A new commitment, dependency, or blocker.
- Need to listen rather than advocate.
- Need to stop, defer, or narrow the discussion.
- Another signal.

## CMEET08 - Interpreting The Changed State

**Question:** What must be agreed after the meeting about what actually changed?

**Response mode:** Select the required interpretations and identify who accepts the final changed state.

**Possible answers:**

- What was learned about client intent.
- Whether trust, candor, or access changed.
- Whether a decision advanced, was protected, or was deferred.
- Whether the relationship objective changed.
- Whether an opportunity judgment or trajectory changed.
- What remains uncertain or disputed among JPM participants.
- Whether action, monitoring, waiting, or stopping is appropriate.
- What may be shared and with whom.
- Another changed-state element.

## CMEET09 - Completion And Follow-Through

**Question:** What must happen before the meeting job is complete rather than merely finished on the calendar?

**Response mode:** Select the minimum completion contract and identify destination owners.

**Possible answers:**

- Interpret and accept the outcome.
- Make explicit commitments and non-commitments.
- Name accountable owners and first next movements.
- Update the relationship judgment.
- Update the opportunity or pipeline state.
- Route new intelligence for validation or monitoring.
- Create or update actions and dependencies.
- Record deliberate non-action and return conditions.
- Begin the first material follow-through step.
- Another completion condition.

## CMEET10 - Scenario Variation

**Question:** Which meeting contexts materially change preparation, participation, or follow-through while preserving the same core job?

**Response mode:** Select material variations and explain what remains invariant.

**Possible answers:**

- Client decision is forming.
- Relationship-sensitive listening or repair.
- Major commitment or negotiation.
- Cross-JPM or multi-product meeting.
- First senior interaction with a stakeholder.
- Crisis, adverse event, or reputation-sensitive meeting.
- Routine strategic review with no acute trigger.
- Virtual, in-person, travel, or short-notice interaction.
- Another scenario.

## CMEET11 - Success And Failure

**Question:** What would prove consequential-meeting work is improving client and franchise outcomes, and what must it avoid?

**Response mode:** Select one or two leading measures, one or two lagging outcomes, and the most important guardrails.

**Possible success measures:**

- Better readiness and less last-minute reconstruction.
- Clearer client intent after the interaction.
- Improved trust, candor, or access.
- Decisions advanced or protected.
- Explicit commitments and reliable ownership.
- Faster first next movement.
- Appropriate deliberate non-action.

**Possible failure modes:**

- Generic preparation without a hypothesis.
- Over-scripted interaction that prevents listening.
- Fragmented JPM posture.
- Unsupported or unauthorized commitments.
- Notes captured without interpretation.
- Unowned follow-through.
- Activity mistaken for client progress.

## CMEET12 - Representative Episode

**Question:** Walk through one consequential client meeting from trigger through first follow-through. Where did preparation, in-room adaptation, interpretation, or ownership materially affect the result?

**Response mode:** Prefer a direct experience and label the meeting type and client context.

**Probes:**

- Why did the meeting warrant senior attention?
- What hypothesis and intended outcome existed beforehand?
- Which uncertainty or alignment issue mattered most?
- What changed during the interaction?
- How were differing interpretations reconciled?
- Which commitment and first next movement followed?

## Proxy Synthesis And Banker Validation Gate

After proxy interviews, synthesize the consequential threshold, readiness and posture contracts, adaptation and changed-state rules, completion and handoff boundary, scenario variations, measures, disagreements, and episode evidence. Current bankers should receive no more than five focused validation questions, including one observed meeting walkthrough and any disputed commitment-authority or completion rule.

# IB ECM Analyst, Junior (A&A Product)

_Persona type: ECM Banker — Junior Analyst & Associate (Product side), J.P. Morgan_

> "My day is never one job — it's a dozen. Feedback to reconcile, a deck to commission, a mandate to set up — all before lunch, all on the same handful of tools."

## Key Insight

Junior ECM bankers operate in a high-pressure, multi-project environment where
much of their time is consumed by manual, repetitive tasks — building decks,
transcribing data, and coordinating logistics — leaving limited capacity for
the higher-order analytical work that drives deal quality and career growth.

## Bio

1–4 years in the industry on the ECM product side. An analyst/associate who
carries the execution detail on live deals and stands up new mandates. Works
across the product silo — bookbuilding support, investor-feedback reconciliation,
deck production, and deal setup — turning the MD's decisions into routed, tracked
work. The engine room of the desk: high volume, high variety, high accuracy bar.

**Career context:**

- Analysts typically join out of undergraduate programs; associates after an MBA or promotion.
- Trajectory: Analyst (2–3 yrs) → Associate (3–4 yrs) → VP, with many pivoting to buyside or corporate development.
- Learns across diverse equity offerings — IPOs, follow-ons, block trades, and convertibles — building fluency in market conditions, investor appetite, and regulatory filing requirements.

## Goals

- Turn around first-draft deliverables (decks, summaries) quickly and accurately
- Reconcile large volumes of investor feedback into themes the team can act on
- Stand up new mandates cleanly — folders, deal team, control-room draft
- Keep every deal record current, routed, and auditable
- Work autonomously across multiple live deals with minimal supervision
- Proactively deliver materials before being asked — anticipating senior bankers' needs
- Gain breadth of experience across diverse deal types and sectors
- Build fluency in equity market dynamics, investor sentiment, and pricing mechanics
- Learn the patterns of the desk and grow toward associate/VP responsibility
- Reduce manual, repetitive coordination so time goes to judgement work

## Needs

- A single queue of outstanding actions to start the day from
- Agent summarisation over 100+ lines of feedback, with the underlying lines one
  click away
- One-motion "approve summary and commission the deck" chains
- Conversational deal setup with revisable proposals (not final on first try)
- Cross-domain precedent and theme search with source attribution
- Governed content generation flagged for compliance before external use
- Clear task ownership and routing across the deal team
- Clear prioritisation of which deliverables take precedence during live deals
- Reusable templates for recurring deliverables (offering comps, targeting lists, roadshow materials)
- Real-time market data (pricing comps, trading multiples, aftermarket performance) close to the work
- Streamlined access to filings and prospectus drafts
- Fast, reliable tooling — quick resolution when tools break mid-workflow

## Pain Points

- A dozen different tasks a day on the same kit — constant context switching
- Manual aggregation of buy-side feedback that doesn't scale during bookbuilding
- Manual data entry and transcription — re-keying data across systems and into slides
- Reconstructing deal context scattered across email, chat, and CRM
- Difficulty finding past materials — rebuilding decks from scratch when prior comps can't be located
- No easy way to discover existing JPMC relationships to a target company or investor
- Getting the agent's first proposal wrong (e.g. wrong-sector deal-team member)
  and needing a fast, low-friction way to correct it
- Compliance and information-barrier checks that interrupt flow when not embedded
- Unclear provenance on generated content — hard to trust without sources
- Burdensome deal tools (e.g. DealWorks) — too many fields and complex permissioning
- Market sensitivity — timelines and pricing/sizing can shift overnight, forcing rapid material updates

## Jobs to be Done

### Deal Execution Support

- Start the day from an actions queue prepared overnight, not a blank screen
- Reconcile 100+ investor feedback lines into themes with the detail preserved
- Approve a summary and commission the follow-on deck job in one motion
- Produce a first-draft deck to a standard template and route it to the team
- Build and maintain financial models — valuation, offering scenarios, dilution analysis

### Deal Setup & Mandate Standup

- Set up a new mandate in chat — folders, deal team, control-room draft
- Correct a wrong agent proposal and re-run it (the repair loop) until right
- Persist approved setup to the deal record and route to the deal team
- Build and maintain working-group lists and investor targeting/allocation models

### Research & Content

- Query precedents for a specific situation and get attributed results
- Run a theme/content search across the permissioned corpus
- Select sources and generate a short client summary, flagged for compliance
- Prepare equity offering comps and precedent-transaction analyses
- Monitor aftermarket trading performance for recent deals and support roadshow logistics

## Tools Used

- **Communication:** Outlook, Microsoft Teams, Zoom
- **Deal management:** DealWorks, Ship, Investix, CFIT
- **Productivity & presentations:** Excel, PowerPoint, PitchPro, Adobe Reader
- **Market data & research:** Bloomberg Terminal, FactSet, AlphaSense, Dealogic, PitchBook, Moody's, TradeWeb
- **Filings:** SEC EDGAR / Bamsec (prospectuses, S-1s)
- **Investor targeting:** Ipreo / IHS Markit (targeting, shareholder ID)
- **CRM / Coverage:** Dash
- **Networking & news:** LinkedIn, financial news platforms

## Opportunities for Product / Design Teams

1. **Automated material retrieval** — quick search and reuse of past decks and offering comps to eliminate "starting from scratch"
2. **Data auto-population** — connect market-data feeds directly into presentation templates to cut manual transcription
3. **Relationship discovery** — surface internal JPMC connections to target companies and investors
4. **Smart scheduling** — streamline coordination for roadshows and investor meetings
5. **Simplified deal tools** — reduce unnecessary fields and streamline permissioning in DealWorks

## Research Notes

- Findings draw primarily from generative interviews with junior bankers across
  GIB (which includes ECM), supplemented with public context on ECM-specific
  workflows where internal research does not differentiate at the sub-product level.
- No study isolates ECM analysts/associates specifically from their DCM or M&A
  peers; quantitative measures (time-on-task, error rates) and ECM-specific user
  quotes were not available in the retrieved sources.

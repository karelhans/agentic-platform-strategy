# IB Coverage MD (M&A / Coverage)

_Persona type: Coverage Managing Director — M&A / Sector Coverage, J.P. Morgan_

> "The best ideas don't wait for me to be at my desk. If I spot the move between meetings, I want to act on it from my phone and hand the rest to the team overnight."

> "Opportunities don't happen, we create them. We help our clients maintain their strategic goal, and we drive business to maintain our leading position as JPMC."

_Data availability: Rich qualitative research is available for this persona from the 2025 GIB Industry Coverage CRM generative interview studies. Findings below are drawn primarily from internal research, supplemented by general industry context._

## Bio

20+ years in the industry. A senior Managing Director who owns the client
relationship for a sector book (e.g. Technology, Healthcare) across a specific
geography (e.g. San Francisco, London, NYC), leading a coverage team and driving
account planning.

Works **proactively** — originating ideas, testing them with clients, and
delegating execution. Acts as the **relationship gatekeeper** and "quarterback"
for the client relationship — bringing in product partners (M&A, ECM, DCM,
Payments, etc.) to serve client needs and leveraging the full JPMorganChase
platform. Often operating from mobile between meetings: reads the franchise's
proprietary insight flow, spots an actionable move (e.g. a consolidation idea),
reaches out, and delegates the follow-through to a junior on the desk. Judgement
and relationships first; execution detail delegated.

_Public context: At bulge-bracket investment banks, a Coverage MD typically has
15–25+ years of experience, manages a portfolio of 20–50+ corporate clients, and
is responsible for originating significant annual revenue (often $10M–$100M+ in
wallet share). They sit within GIB Coverage and serve as the "quarterback" for
the client relationship._

## Goals

- Originate high-conviction, proactive ideas for the client book
- Move on an insight quickly — insight to outreach in minutes, from anywhere
- Efficiently manage, prioritize, and tier client relationships and coverage
- Drive revenue opportunities and strategic account planning
- Delegate execution cleanly and watch it progress without micromanaging
- Strengthen client relationships through timely, differentiated advice — especially with Tier 1 clients
- Maintain alignment with industry heads and senior management
- Bring in product partners to deliver the full JP Morgan platform (advisory, payments, hedging, FX, debt capital markets)
- Keep JPM ahead of competitors on proactive coverage

## Needs

- A composed morning **Brief** — meetings, open actions, one highlighted signal —
  tailored to this banker's book
- Insight alerts that open into a scenario view and a drafted client outreach
- The consequence of an action stated **at the point of sending**
- One-tap delegation of a deck to an agent, with visible status afterward
- Centralized, up-to-date client coverage information and dashboards of current assignments
- Efficient client tiering (e.g. a "3 by 3" grid) and searchable, accurate records
- Better information sharing and connection tools, balanced with confidentiality
- Integration with Outlook for communication and sharing with senior bankers
- Mobile-first ergonomics — everything doable from a phone between meetings, even without WiFi

## Pain Points

- The best ideas surface when away from the desk, on mobile
- Inconsistent connectivity and tool gaps across regions (esp. EMEA)
- Losing time between spotting an insight and acting on it
- Delegated work disappearing into a black box with no status
- Balancing relationship management with internal execution demands
- Heavy reliance on manual, fragmented tools (Excel, Outlook, OneNote) — version-control issues and lost institutional knowledge
- Poor adoption and input capture in CRM systems (e.g. Dash) — incomplete data and low trust in accuracy
- Outdated or hard-to-find coverage information — redundant outreach and client confusion
- Manual compilation of client lists and meeting scheduling — a major time sink
- Applications that don't match the banker's mental model — unintuitive navigation, low adoption

## Jobs to be Done

### Orient

- Open a composed morning Brief and orient the day at a glance
- Manage and tier client coverage lists (by geography and priority)
- Connect client needs with opportunities across large volumes of information

### Act

- Tap an insight alert → scenario view → drafted client outreach
- Send or defer, with the consequence stated at send
- Track next steps and revenue opportunities (IPOs, buy/sell sides, debt transactions)
- Take and store meeting notes (rarely shared unless requested)

### Delegate

- Delegate the deck to the agent and monitor status ("DRAFTING")
- Hand off overnight to the deal team; pick up the thread the next day
- Collaborate with product partners for account planning and follow up on outstanding items

## Tools Used

- **Communication:** Outlook (mobile), Microsoft Teams, Zoom, Telephone, Movius, iMeet
- **Coverage / CRM:** Dash (mainly to check coverage/contact points), internal insight/coverage flow, DealWorks (assignments)
- **Client management & notes:** Excel (client lists, account plans, tiering), OneNote, PowerPoint
- **Market data & research:** Bloomberg (mobile), FactSet/Bamsec, PitchBook, Dealogic, AlphaSense, TradingView, Moody's, TradeWeb
- **Networking & intelligence:** LinkedIn, financial news platforms
- **AI & workflow:** LLM Suite, Ship

## Key Insight

> The Coverage MD's workflow is **relationship-first and highly personal** — yet
> the tools available are overwhelmingly transactional and administrative. The gap
> between how senior bankers _think_ about client management (tiered
> relationships, strategic opportunities, warm introductions) and how existing
> systems _force_ them to work (data entry, manual list compilation, fragmented
> notes) is the core design challenge.

## Opportunities & Actionable Implications

- **CRM redesign** should mirror the MD's mental model: client tiers, next-best-action prompts, and revenue opportunity pipelines — not data-entry forms.
- **Mobile-first interaction logging** could dramatically improve data capture without burdening senior bankers.
- **AI-assisted meeting prep** (auto-surfacing recent interactions, deal history, news) addresses the "connecting clients to opportunities" job to be done.
- **Outlook integration** is non-negotiable — email remains the senior banker's primary workspace.

## Journeys

- **Primary:** J3 — The acquisition idea (`acquisition-idea`), on **mobile**:
  orient → act → delegate. Continues overnight as J4 on the junior's desktop.
